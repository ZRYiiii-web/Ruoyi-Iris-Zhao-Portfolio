DribbleIQ

From movement to meaningful feedback.

An intelligent basketball training concept connecting wearable sensing, a companion app and computer vision. The work explores how players can understand their performance and translate feedback into practice.

01 / DRIBBLEIQ

The question

Practising alone can make progress difficult to judge. This project begins with amateur basketball players and the gap between repeating a movement and knowing what to improve. Interviews and movement analysis explore rhythm, posture and ball control.

02 / DRIBBLEIQ

A connected training experience

The concept brings sensing, training guidance and reflection into one experience. Wearable explorations focus on the hand, elbow and back. The app gives training records and feedback a place in the player’s ongoing practice.

03 / DRIBBLEIQ

Making feedback usable

The app develops a visual language for training, performance reports and continued participation. The central design question is how to make detected movement understandable enough to inform the next session.

04 / DRIBBLEIQ

From concept to implementation

BounceCoach is the computer-vision training work shown alongside the wider DribbleIQ concept. It combines person and ball detection, pose analysis and generated training reports. This research was accepted as a poster at IEEE VR 2026. The experience shifted my focus toward feedback that people can understand and act on.
